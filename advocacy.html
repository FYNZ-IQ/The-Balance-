Advocacy & Documentation

From individual cases to lasting change

Every case teaches us something about how the law and the mental health system work, and where they fail. We document what we learn, carefully and with consent, and use it to push for change.

Documentation

How we document human rights violations

When a client's experience points to a wider problem, and only with their informed consent, we record it. We remove anything that could identify them. We store records securely. We never publish an individual account.

Over time, these records show patterns: how protection orders are enforced, how custody decisions treat children, what happens to mental health inside prisons. Patterns are what change policy.

Advocacy priorities

What we are working to change

Family law and mediation

Family courts and mediation processes that take trauma into account, and that treat children as people with their own interests.

Response to gender-based violence

Protection that actually protects: enforcement of orders, trauma-informed police and court practice, and care alongside legal process.

Mental health in detention

Recognition of the mental health needs of incarcerated people, and access to care and legal help inside prisons.

Child protection

Custody and protection systems that put a child's safety and wellbeing first, with therapy available as part of the process.

Mental health as a human right

Recognition in law and practice that psychological wellbeing is part of justice, not separate from it.

Integration as standard

A legal aid and mental health system in Ethiopia where combining the two is normal, not exceptional.

How we advocate

Our methods

Strategic litigation

Taking selected cases through the courts where a decision could protect many people, always with the client's full consent and interests first.

Policy submissions and briefs

Evidence-based submissions to ministries, courts, and parliament, drawing on our documentation.

Training and partnerships

Working with courts, police, prisons, health services, and other organisations to spread trauma-informed, integrated practice.

Get Help

All our services are free of charge. We never ask the people we help to pay for anything. Legal aid, therapy, home visits, prison visits, and the hotline all cost nothing.
